# Format-specific editorial rules

Load when generating prose for a specific deliverable shape and you need the per-format editorial notes that go beyond structure. The fillable templates live in `templates/OUTPUT_TEMPLATES.md`; the rules below are about *what to say* once the template is in front of you.

## Essay / assignment response

Make sure the introduction answers the prompt. Use storytelling as a framing device, not a substitute for analysis. Keep thesis, argument, evidence, and conclusion visible. Preserve academic integrity and required citations.

## One-pager opener / PRD problem statement

Open on customer friction observed in evidence, not on framing. Name JTBD or segment in concrete terms. Cite the evidence inline (interview ID, dashboard link). State the bet — not a wish list — by the second or third paragraph.

## Exec memo / decision memo

First sentence states the decision or ask. Followed by why-now and the chosen frame. Options considered named. Risk acknowledged in one sentence, not buried. No generic upbeat conclusion.

## Release notes

User-language, not internal-language. *"X works now"* beats *"the X experience has been enhanced."* Skip the "we are thrilled" opening. One section per change, with a one-line "why this matters to you" if the change is non-obvious.

## Article / blog

Strong lede, beats as sections, end with practical insight or memorable final line. Examples and transitions are not optional.

## Video script

Time-coded or scene-based. Hook in the first 5–10 seconds. Visual cues, voiceover, on-screen text, pacing. Avoid dense exposition.

## Slide storyline

One idea per slide. Slide titles are claims, not labels. Speaker notes carry density. Convert dense content into scenes, contrasts, decisions. For a full deck (QBR, exec review, kickoff, pitch), the per-slide contract, the slide budget, and the render handoff live in `references/deck-storyline.md`.

## Case study

Structure around the protagonist's change. Separate claim, evidence, and interpretation. Do not fabricate results — use `[NEEDS METRIC]` when proof is missing.

## Lesson / teaching story

Open on a relatable problem or puzzle. Curiosity → explanation → example → practice → reflection. Student-facing language when useful.

## Social sequence

Each post carries one beat. Clear progression, not repeated summaries. Adapt CTA to platform. No clickbait unless explicitly requested.
