## 2026-09-16: Review: the grader's direction, and void short-circuits the summary

Both land. On the first, you are right that the second half was circular and that the two conversions were missing: nothing said how the grader's output becomes up, down or flat, and nothing said what magnitude counts as a direction rather than flat. Meanwhile the human side had just acquired a discrete procedure, so the report could still pick the discretisation after seeing the delta. I took your second option, grader direction from per-pair pass and fail movement under the same margin rule, for three reasons. It introduces no threshold: PASS_THRESHOLD in scripts/grade_evals.py is already the binariser, and agreement() already uses pass_rate at or above it as "the grader passed this run", so the magnitude question is answered by a rule that was pre-registered long before this section existed. The alternative, sign of the paired delta, would reintroduce exactly the false precision the section two above already refuses: the per-skill delta is a mean of four to six rates against a mean of four to six more, and taking its sign would make 0.004 an improvement. And the two sides become comparable, since both are then pair-level directions over the same margin rule and the same pairs. A grader pair is up when the without_skill run falls below the threshold and the with_skill run lands at or above it, down for the reverse, flat when both fall the same side. The grader has no critical-failure signal, because a critical failure is something a labeler records, so it has no conflict and no unresolved state of its own; an unresolved pair is dropped from both sides, so the two directions run over exactly the same pairs. Margin and holds are now defined once, for either side, and step 2 requires both directions to hold. That closes on the grader side the hole your third finding closed on the human side: with a human margin of three and a grader margin of one against two unresolved pairs, the unresolved set could have flipped the agreement without touching the direction above it. Agreement is stated as a relation over all nine combinations: exactly two are disagreement, human up with grader down and human down with grader up. Every other pairing agrees, including any where either side is flat, and the reason is written down rather than assumed: the grader crosses a fixed threshold while the human changes category, so a coarser instrument standing still does not contradict a movement the finer one saw, while opposite directions do. Strict equality was the alternative and it would make a useful signal nearly unreachable, because weak to good with both ends above the threshold is flat on the grader by construction; say the word and I will make it equality. One honest sentence went in with it: agreement() compares a level per run and feeds grader_disagreement_rate and investigate_grader, so the paired direction is a construction of this protocol that nothing in the runner computes, and the option of computing it in aggregate_benchmark is registered on the B46 enforcement bullet rather than left floating in prose. On the second finding the conflict was real and the fix is the ordering you proposed. A void harness iteration now short-circuits the summary: there is no toolkit-level reading at all, and the void iteration is re-run under a new pre-registration first. Two void iterations are not matching verdicts either, because void is not a verdict. Only after both harnesses carry a valid verdict are they compared, the same valid verdict is the toolkit-level reading, and different valid verdicts give inconclusive with nothing in the difference attributed to a harness. One addition of my own, found while checking the first finding and declared as mine: aggregate_benchmark accepts an entry with only one configuration, so a run that failed and was never recorded leaves a pair that matched neither half of the contaminated definition, which covered a run that failed and was retried. A pair missing either configuration is now contaminated, and therefore unresolved. Checked by extending the model from the previous round and running it: the threshold crossings in both directions and the two both-sides-the-same cases, the nine agreement combinations with exactly two disagreeing, a grader margin that fails to hold sending the skill to step 2, a pair with a missing configuration reading as unresolved, and the sixteen harness combinations with void short-circuiting exactly the seven it appears in while two inconclusive verdicts still read as inconclusive. ## 2026-09-16: Review: the flat run, pair direction, and the unresolved set

All three findings land, and the first is a policy change I made without saying so. The approved framing had "no material paired improvement" as a standalone negative trigger; writing step 1 I conjoined it twice, with a material regression and with a cost rise, so a run in which nothing moves at all satisfies neither clause, falls to the residual and reads as inconclusive. The length clause made it worse: with zero improvements "every material improvement is explained by length" is vacuously true on the strict reading and false on the colloquial one, so the same flat run had two available verdicts depending on how a reader took the sentence, which is the post-hoc freedom the section exists to remove. The negative step now carries the approved standalone trigger as its own line, and the length trigger requires at least one material improvement before it can fire. Pair direction is now total. It is built from two signals so exclusivity is by construction rather than by careful wording: the category signal from the human verdict, the critical-failure signal from the pre-registered set. A pair is unresolved when the two point opposite ways, when the labelers split so there is no consolidated category, or when it is contaminated; otherwise it is up, down or flat. Your conflict case, with_skill dropping good to weak while a critical failure disappears, is unresolved rather than up, because treating that as an improvement hides a regression a human saw. The two-signal form also settles a case the four clauses alone left double-covered, one critical failure appearing while another disappears with the category flat, which reads down. Unresolved pairs are now read as a set instead of one at a time. A skill's margin is its up pairs minus its down pairs, and its direction holds only when the margin, ignoring sign, is greater than the number of unresolved pairs, the worst case being all of them falling the same way. Your example, two up, none down, two flat and two split, gives margin two against two unresolved, so the direction does not hold and the run is inconclusive; three up with one split holds. Contamination and splits stop being separate one-pair clauses because both are unresolved pairs and the margin rule covers them collectively. Two consequences I am declaring rather than burying. Cost and latency no longer carry a verdict at all: the one case where they did, a run in which nothing improved, is now the standalone negative trigger, so the cost clause decided nothing there and anywhere else it would have let a second variable overrule the quality reading; they stay in the report. And the readability check had to move above the absence-of-improvement trigger, which splits the old step 1 in two, because a run whose pairs are all unresolved has no evidence at all and the old order read it as negative, the same absence-as-evidence defect in a different place. A critical-failure regression still precedes everything, and it now ignores contaminated pairs. Checked by modelling the procedure and running it: the nine-cell signal grid maps onto exactly four pair states, the flat run is negative once, the fully unresolved run is inconclusive, your six-pair example is inconclusive, three up with one split holds, the three cases from the previous round still map to useful, negative and inconclusive, and 5832 swept states each return exactly one verdict. The two active changelog entries that stated the superseded steps are marked as superseded and named, rather than rewritten as the previous round rewrote its predecessor, because a changelog entry is the record of what a commit did. Against main the protocol file is additive, so nothing in the instrument, tag, smoke or pre-registration contract merged in #26 is weakened. Full docs/REPO_HEALTH.md battery green before the commit. ## 2026-09-16: Review: the aggregate verdict becomes an ordered procedure with terms fixed before the run

Superseded by "Review: the flat run, pair direction, and the unresolved set" (2026-09-16), which kept the ordered procedure and changed four things in it: the negative step split in two around the readability check, the standalone "no skill improves materially" trigger came back as its own line, a pair direction became total with a fourth state for conflicting signals, and cost and latency stopped carrying a verdict at all. What follows is kept as the record of what this commit did, not as the current rule. All three findings land, and the first two defeat the purpose of the section I wrote: it existed to remove post-hoc freedom and it left three kinds of it. Your counter-examples reproduce against my own text. Two skills improving with a non-critical regression in the third satisfies all five Useful conditions and also the Inconclusive "mixed" condition, because "no skill regresses on critical failures" says nothing about a non-critical regression. Critical failures rising with mixed skills satisfies Negative and Inconclusive at once. One skill improving with two unchanged, clean controls, no disagreement and flat cost satisfies none of the three: Useful needs two skills, nothing is mixed or ambiguous so Inconclusive does not fire, and there is a material improvement so Negative does not either. The section is now an ordered procedure, first match wins, with a residual step, so one evidence state maps to exactly one verdict. Step 0 voids an iteration that broke rule 1 or the tag binding, and void is not a result. Step 1 is negative signal on any of: a critical-failure regression; no material improvement alongside a material regression; every material improvement explained by length; no material improvement alongside a cost or latency rise. Step 2 is inconclusive on any of: an unreadable skill; a contaminated pair that would change a direction; a direction resting on a single split pair. Step 3 is useful signal only if all hold: two of the three skills improve materially, no negative control is worse, grader and human agree on every skill. Step 4 makes everything else inconclusive. Your three cases now trace to Useful, Negative and Inconclusive respectively, each exactly once. The load-bearing terms are defined before the run by something observable in the recorded run, not judged in the report. Movement is a change of human verdict category or the appearance or removal of a pre-registered critical failure, so a grader score that moves without changing the category is not movement. A skill improves materially when more of its pairs move up than down and none moves down into a critical failure. An improvement is explained by length when the labeler's own recorded reason cites added length or the assertion's wording rather than the behaviour it names. Grader and human agree when the directions match or the difference does not change the direction. A skill is unreadable when investigate_grader fires and the human verdicts do not settle the direction. A pair is contaminated when its run failed and was retried, or its envelope does not match the pre-registered harness version, model or isolation configuration. You are right that "a judgement recorded with its evidence" documented the judgement without pre-registering the rule behind it, which is exactly where the verdict could still be moved. Cost and latency stop being a free variable: they are always reported, and they reach a verdict only through step 1, whose quality condition is already that nothing improved materially. A cost rise next to a real quality gain is reported and does not make the run negative. On the third finding I picked per harness and said why rather than leaving it open. The comparison is paired inside one harness, the two harnesses run different models, and the protocol already forbids attributing a difference to the harness when the models differ; a single pooled verdict would do precisely that. So each harness iteration gets its own verdict, the summary carries both side by side, a toolkit-level decision requires the same verdict from both, and two differing harness verdicts are not evidence about the harnesses. The active changelog entry carried the old three-bullet semantics and is corrected to the same shape. Against main the protocol file is 66 added lines and no deletions, so nothing from the instrument, tag, smoke or pre-registration contract merged in #26 is touched; the only replaced lines are the ones I added in 18800d0. Full docs/REPO_HEALTH.md battery green before the commit. Documentation and records only.
