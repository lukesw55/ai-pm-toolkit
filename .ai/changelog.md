## 2026-09-16: Review: the critical-failure guardrail under unresolved evidence

Both land, and the first is the same class I closed for negative controls one commit ago, one step to the side. I wrote "any pair that is not contaminated" into the step 1 veto in an earlier round for the right reason, that contaminated evidence must not convict, and never wrote the mirror of it: contaminated evidence must not acquit either. Your six-pair case reproduces against the text. Four resolved up, one resolved flat, one contaminated or missing a configuration, control resolved and clear, margin four against one unresolved, so the direction holds at four greater than one, the skill improves materially, and with a second skill in the same shape step 4 returns useful while the pair that might have carried a new critical failure is exactly the one nothing read. Robustness of a sign is not observation of a veto. The guardrail is now explicit and required by step 4 beside the negative controls: the critical-failure guardrail is clear when every pair in the iteration establishes whether a pre-registered critical failure appeared under with_skill and none did. Establishing it is defined by what the recorded run contains rather than by judgement: both configurations recorded and labelled, and the labelers of the with_skill run agreeing on which pre-registered critical failures it carries. They may still split on the verdict category, which is a different question. A contaminated pair never establishes it, because a missing configuration leaves nothing to compare against and a mismatched envelope means the recorded output is not the one the instrument describes. I took the narrow guardrail you preferred over requiring no unresolved pair at all, and the difference is declared rather than left for a reader to work out: one contaminated pair in any skill now keeps step 4 from firing, while a pair unresolved only because the labelers split on the category, or because its two signals conflict, still leaves useful reachable, since in both of those the critical-failure state is on the record and a conflict in which a critical failure appeared has already fired step 1. On the second point the sentence was mine and it overstated. BACKTICK_PATH captures the path in group one and leaves the fragment outside it, and check_backtick_paths tests only that the path exists, so the validator proves docs/EVAL_PROTOCOL.md is there and never that the anchor is spelled right. The sentence now says the file path and only the file path is checked, that a protocol moved or renamed turns the validator red, and that the anchor is part of the reference the tag freezes rather than something CI enforces. I did not register anchor validation anywhere, and the reason is the difference from last round: the grader direction went on the B46 bullet because the protocol requires a computation nothing performs, which is a standing gap, while here the protocol requires nothing of CI once the sentence is accurate, so there is nothing to track. Checked by extending the model and running it, and two of my own expectations were wrong rather than the protocol. Your case is inconclusive and not useful; the same shape with nothing contaminated is useful; a pair unresolved only by a split, and one unresolved by conflicting signals, both still allow useful, which is what the narrow guardrail buys; one contaminated pair in a skill that is not the improving one blocks; an observed critical-failure regression is still negative and a direction that does not hold still reaches step 2 first; a wholly flat run is still negative, while a flat skill that also carries a contaminated pair is inconclusive at step 2, because margin zero against one unresolved cannot hold, which is correct and was my expectation that was wrong; the unresolved negative control still blocks; and 3888 swept states each return exactly one verdict, after I fixed a sweep that held one skill fixed and could not reach the negative branch at all. ## 2026-09-16: Review: the negative-control guardrail, and pre-registration by reference

Step 4 required that no negative control be materially worse, and the term was defined as a property of the pair, but the only robustness rule I added asks whether the unresolved set could change a skill's direction. Those are different properties. Your case reproduces against the text: five pairs, three resolved up, one flat, the negative control split or contaminated, human margin three against one unresolved, so the direction holds at three greater than one, the grader side can hold too, the skill counts as materially improved, and the protocol still cannot establish that the control did not get worse, because that pair carries no consolidated verdict. So the control pair is one of that skill's six, five or four and lands in the same unresolved set as any other. I took your second wording, which keeps the condition local to step 4. The per-pair term is now a negative-control pair is materially worse, and a new term carries the guardrail: the negative controls are clear, meaning every negative-control pair in the iteration is resolved and none of them is worse. Being resolved is half the condition and the reason is written down rather than left to be inferred: a split or contaminated control leaves the direction intact while the guarantee that pair exists to provide goes unobserved, so the run would claim something its evidence does not carry. Step 4 uses the new term, so an unresolved control stops step 4 from firing and the iteration reaches the residual, which is inconclusive. That is what your first option would have produced, without adding a step. On the second point you are right that same file was not literally true: rule 3 defines the pre-registration artefact as docs/benchmarks/<measured-iteration>/pre-registration.md while the aggregate procedure lives in docs/EVAL_PROTOCOL.md, and duplicating a hundred lines would be worse than the imprecision. The opening now says the run-level verdict is pre-registered by reference rather than by copy: the procedure lives in the protocol, the pre-registration file names it in its criteria block, and the annotated tag freezes that version of the protocol together with the criteria. The criteria block gains the field you offered, and one detail of it changed after measurement. You wrote the value as EVAL_PROTOCOL.md with a fragment; BACKTICK_PATH in scripts/validate_repo.py only follows a backticked token that holds a directory separator and no whitespace, and it drops the fragment before resolving, so the whole field inside one pair of backticks is never checked and a bare file name is never checked either. The field is therefore written with the key in plain text and the value alone in backticks, carrying the docs prefix, which is the only one of the three forms the checker resolves; I ran all three to find out. A pre-registration file under docs/benchmarks is scanned by that check like any other document, since only the generated proposal cards skip, so the reference is verified on every run of the validator rather than trusted. ## 2026-09-16: Review: the grader's direction, and void short-circuits the summary

Both land. On the first, you are right that the second half was circular and that the two conversions were missing: nothing said how the grader's output becomes up, down or flat, and nothing said what magnitude counts as a direction rather than flat. Meanwhile the human side had just acquired a discrete procedure, so the report could still pick the discretisation after seeing the delta. I took your second option, grader direction from per-pair pass and fail movement under the same margin rule, for three reasons. It introduces no threshold: PASS_THRESHOLD in scripts/grade_evals.py is already the binariser, and agreement() already uses pass_rate at or above it as "the grader passed this run", so the magnitude question is answered by a rule that was pre-registered long before this section existed. The alternative, sign of the paired delta, would reintroduce exactly the false precision the section two above already refuses: the per-skill delta is a mean of four to six rates against a mean of four to six more, and taking its sign would make 0.004 an improvement. And the two sides become comparable, since both are then pair-level directions over the same margin rule and the same pairs. A grader pair is up when the without_skill run falls below the threshold and the with_skill run lands at or above it, down for the reverse, flat when both fall the same side. The grader has no critical-failure signal, because a critical failure is something a labeler records, so it has no conflict and no unresolved state of its own; an unresolved pair is dropped from both sides, so the two directions run over exactly the same pairs. Margin and holds are now defined once, for either side, and step 2 requires both directions to hold. That closes on the grader side the hole your third finding closed on the human side: with a human margin of three and a grader margin of one against two unresolved pairs, the unresolved set could have flipped the agreement without touching the direction above it. Agreement is stated as a relation over all nine combinations: exactly two are disagreement, human up with grader down and human down with grader up. Every other pairing agrees, including any where either side is flat, and the reason is written down rather than assumed: the grader crosses a fixed threshold while the human changes category, so a coarser instrument standing still does not contradict a movement the finer one saw, while opposite directions do. Strict equality was the alternative and it would make a useful signal nearly unreachable, because weak to good with both ends above the threshold is flat on the grader by construction; say the word and I will make it equality. One honest sentence went in with it: agreement() compares a level per run and feeds grader_disagreement_rate and investigate_grader, so the paired direction is a construction of this protocol that nothing in the runner computes, and the option of computing it in aggregate_benchmark is registered on the B46 enforcement bullet rather than left floating in prose. On the second finding the conflict was real and the fix is the ordering you proposed. A void harness iteration now short-circuits the summary: there is no toolkit-level reading at all, and the void iteration is re-run under a new pre-registration first. Two void iterations are not matching verdicts either, because void is not a verdict. Only after both harnesses carry a valid verdict are they compared, the same valid verdict is the toolkit-level reading, and different valid verdicts give inconclusive with nothing in the difference attributed to a harness. One addition of my own, found while checking the first finding and declared as mine: aggregate_benchmark accepts an entry with only one configuration, so a run that failed and was never recorded leaves a pair that matched neither half of the contaminated definition, which covered a run that failed and was retried. A pair missing either configuration is now contaminated, and therefore unresolved. Checked by extending the model from the previous round and running it: the threshold crossings in both directions and the two both-sides-the-same cases, the nine agreement combinations with exactly two disagreeing, a grader margin that fails to hold sending the skill to step 2, a pair with a missing configuration reading as unresolved, and the sixteen harness combinations with void short-circuiting exactly the seven it appears in while two inconclusive verdicts still read as inconclusive. The previous round's sweep still passes unchanged. Full docs/REPO_HEALTH.md battery green before the commit. Documentation and records only.
