## 2026-09-16: Review: the flat run, pair direction, and the unresolved set

All three findings land, and the first is a policy change I made without saying so. The approved framing had "no material paired improvement" as a standalone negative trigger; writing step 1 I conjoined it twice, with a material regression and with a cost rise, so a run in which nothing moves at all satisfies neither clause, falls to the residual and reads as inconclusive. The length clause made it worse: with zero improvements "every material improvement is explained by length" is vacuously true on the strict reading and false on the colloquial one, so the same flat run had two available verdicts depending on how a reader took the sentence, which is the post-hoc freedom the section exists to remove. The negative step now carries the approved standalone trigger as its own line, and the length trigger requires at least one material improvement before it can fire. Pair direction is now total. It is built from two signals so exclusivity is by construction rather than by careful wording: the category signal from the human verdict, the critical-failure signal from the pre-registered set. A pair is unresolved when the two point opposite ways, when the labelers split so there is no consolidated category, or when it is contaminated; otherwise it is up, down or flat. Your conflict case, with_skill dropping good to weak while a critical failure disappears, is unresolved rather than up, because treating that as an improvement hides a regression a human saw. The two-signal form also settles a case the four clauses alone left double-covered, one critical failure appearing while another disappears with the category flat, which reads down. Unresolved pairs are now read as a set instead of one at a time. A skill's margin is its up pairs minus its down pairs, and its direction holds only when the margin, ignoring sign, is greater than the number of unresolved pairs, the worst case being all of them falling the same way. Your example, two up, none down, two flat and two split, gives margin two against two unresolved, so the direction does not hold and the run is inconclusive; three up with one split holds. Contamination and splits stop being separate one-pair clauses because both are unresolved pairs and the margin rule covers them collectively. Two consequences I am declaring rather than burying. Cost and latency no longer carry a verdict at all: the one case where they did, a run in which nothing improved, is now the standalone negative trigger, so the cost clause decided nothing there and anywhere else it would have let a second variable overrule the quality reading; they stay in the report. And the readability check had to move above the absence-of-improvement trigger, which splits the old step 1 in two, because a run whose pairs are all unresolved has no evidence at all and the old order read it as negative, the same absence-as-evidence defect in a different place. A critical-failure regression still precedes everything, and it now ignores contaminated pairs. Checked by modelling the procedure and running it: the nine-cell signal grid maps onto exactly four pair states, the flat run is negative once, the fully unresolved run is inconclusive, your six-pair example is inconclusive, three up with one split holds, the three cases from the previous round still map to useful, negative and inconclusive, and 5832 swept states each return exactly one verdict. The two active changelog entries that stated the superseded steps are marked as superseded and named, rather than rewritten as the previous round rewrote its predecessor, because a changelog entry is the record of what a commit did. Against main the protocol file is additive, so nothing in the instrument, tag, smoke or pre-registration contract merged in #26 is weakened. Full docs/REPO_HEALTH.md battery green before the commit. Documentation and records only.

## 2026-09-16: Review: the aggregate verdict becomes an ordered procedure with terms fixed before the run

Superseded by "Review: the flat run, pair direction, and the unresolved set" (2026-09-16), which kept the ordered procedure and changed four things in it: the negative step split in two around the readability check, the standalone "no skill improves materially" trigger came back as its own line, a pair direction became total with a fourth state for conflicting signals, and cost and latency stopped carrying a verdict at all. What follows is kept as the record of what this commit did, not as the current rule. All three findings land, and the first two defeat the purpose of the section I wrote: it existed to remove post-hoc freedom and it left three kinds of it. Your counter-examples reproduce against my own text. Two skills improving with a non-critical regression in the third satisfies all five Useful conditions and also the Inconclusive "mixed" condition, because "no skill regresses on critical failures" says nothing about a non-critical regression. Critical failures rising with mixed skills satisfies Negative and Inconclusive at once. One skill improving with two unchanged, clean controls, no disagreement and flat cost satisfies none of the three: Useful needs two skills, nothing is mixed or ambiguous so Inconclusive does not fire, and there is a material improvement so Negative does not either. The section is now an ordered procedure, first match wins, with a residual step, so one evidence state maps to exactly one verdict. Step 0 voids an iteration that broke rule 1 or the tag binding, and void is not a result. Step 1 is negative signal on any of: a critical-failure regression; no material improvement alongside a material regression; every material improvement explained by length; no material improvement alongside a cost or latency rise. Step 2 is inconclusive on any of: an unreadable skill; a contaminated pair that would change a direction; a direction resting on a single split pair. Step 3 is useful signal only if all hold: two of the three skills improve materially, no negative control is worse, grader and human agree on every skill. Step 4 makes everything else inconclusive. Your three cases now trace to Useful, Negative and Inconclusive respectively, each exactly once. The load-bearing terms are defined before the run by something observable in the recorded run, not judged in the report. Movement is a change of human verdict category or the appearance or removal of a pre-registered critical failure, so a grader score that moves without changing the category is not movement. A skill improves materially when more of its pairs move up than down and none moves down into a critical failure. An improvement is explained by length when the labeler's own recorded reason cites added length or the assertion's wording rather than the behaviour it names. Grader and human agree when the directions match or the difference does not change the direction. A skill is unreadable when investigate_grader fires and the human verdicts do not settle the direction. A pair is contaminated when its run failed and was retried, or its envelope does not match the pre-registered harness version, model or isolation configuration. You are right that "a judgement recorded with its evidence" documented the judgement without pre-registering the rule behind it, which is exactly where the verdict could still be moved. Cost and latency stop being a free variable: they are always reported, and they reach a verdict only through step 1, whose quality condition is already that nothing improved materially. A cost rise next to a real quality gain is reported and does not make the run negative. On the third finding I picked per harness and said why rather than leaving it open. The comparison is paired inside one harness, the two harnesses run different models, and the protocol already forbids attributing a difference to the harness when the models differ; a single pooled verdict would do precisely that. So each harness iteration gets its own verdict, the summary carries both side by side, a toolkit-level decision requires the same verdict from both, and two differing harness verdicts are not evidence about the harnesses. The active changelog entry carried the old three-bullet semantics and is corrected to the same shape. Against main the protocol file is 66 added lines and no deletions, so nothing from the instrument, tag, smoke or pre-registration contract merged in #26 is touched; the only replaced lines are the ones I added in 18800d0. Full docs/REPO_HEALTH.md battery green before the commit. Documentation and records only.

## 2026-09-16: EVAL_PROTOCOL: how to read the pilot as a whole

Superseded by "Review: the flat run, pair direction, and the unresolved set" (2026-09-16), which kept the ordered procedure and changed four things in it: the negative step split in two around the readability check, the standalone "no skill improves materially" trigger came back as its own line, a pair direction became total with a fourth state for conflicting signals, and cost and latency stopped carrying a verdict at all. What follows is kept as the record of what this commit did, not as the current rule. Rule 3 pre-registers a decision per pilot skill, and the report section requires disagreement rates per skill and overall, but nothing said what the pilot as a whole means. Deciding that once the results are in carries the same defect the per-skill pre-registration exists to prevent: the verdict ends up describing the results. The run-level verdict is now pre-registered in the same file and the same commit as the per-skill conditions, before any measured output exists. Three verdicts reached by an ordered procedure, first match wins, so one evidence state maps to exactly one verdict: step 0 voids an iteration that broke rule 1 or the tag binding; step 1 is negative signal on a critical-failure regression, on no material improvement alongside a material regression, on every improvement being explained by length, or on no material improvement alongside a cost or latency rise; step 2 is inconclusive on an unreadable skill, on a contaminated pair that would change a direction, or on a direction resting on a single split pair; step 3 is useful signal only if at least two of the three skills improve materially, no negative control is worse and grader and human agree on every skill; step 4 makes everything else inconclusive, so the procedure is total. No line in it is a statistical test. Every load-bearing term is defined before the run by something observable in the recorded run rather than judged afterwards: movement is a change of human verdict category or the appearance or removal of a pre-registered critical failure; a skill improves materially when more pairs move up than down with none moving down into a critical failure; an improvement is explained by length when the labeler's own recorded reason cites length or assertion wording; a skill is unreadable when investigate_grader fires and the human verdicts do not settle the direction; a pair is contaminated when its run failed and was retried or its envelope does not match the pre-registered harness version, model or isolation configuration. Cost and latency are always reported and can only trigger a verdict through step 1, where the quality condition is already that nothing improved. The report still names which pairs moved and in which direction, per skill, against 6, 5 or 4. The verdict is per harness, never pooled across the two, because the models differ and the protocol forbids attributing a difference to the harness; a toolkit-level decision needs the same verdict from both, and two differing harness verdicts are not evidence about the harnesses. Two sentences carry the weight. Inconclusive is a legitimate outcome and the likely one at this size, not a failure to be argued away. And it does not license a second reading of the same outputs under different criteria: changing the criteria means a new pre-registration and a new iteration, which is rule 1 rather than a new rule. The diff is additive against main, so nothing in the pilot contract is weakened. The real pre-registration file for a measured iteration is still created on the pilot machine, not here.
