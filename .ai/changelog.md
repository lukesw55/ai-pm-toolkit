## 2026-09-16: B42 review: a false accept targets a check that can still flip it

Your first finding is right, and it lands on the correction from the last round rather than on the original code. I fixed the false sentence and inherited the opposite error: a check that already failed became the thing the card told you to tighten. The arithmetic is the whole argument. A check that returns false contributes nothing to the pass rate, so tightening it leaves 4 of 5 exactly where it was; the binarised pass survives and the false accept stays a false accept. The rate only moves when a check that passes today starts rejecting the output, or when a new check enters. implicated() now keeps the two apart. For a false accept, labels holds candidates drawn from the checks that passed, and already_failing holds the ones that did not. A candidate is a passing check that also passes this eval's own bad fixture, or failing that its keyword-only fixture, because a check a wrong answer already satisfies is the one least likely to be checking behaviour. With neither, labels is empty and the card says there is no mechanical candidate rather than naming one anyway: the gap is a check that is missing, and the reviewer writes it. For a false reject nothing changes, since there the checks that fired are the ones to loosen. assertion_change() loses its special case and takes its targets from labels in both categories. It gains checks_to_flip, the number of currently passing checks that would have to fail for the binarised result to change. That number is the difference between a card that asks for one assertion and one that admits a single change cannot get there: at 4 of 5 one is enough, because 3 of 5 is 0.60, and at 9 of 10 one is not, because 8 of 10 still clears 0.80. The card says this in order: how many passed and that the pass came from the threshold; which already fail and that tightening them leaves the result where it is, with the count of what would actually have to move; the candidates and why they are candidates; then the labeler's reason. The failing checks stay named, because a block where a real failure still clears the threshold is worth a second look on its own, but they are no longer a recommendation. Worth recording: against the repo's real assertions the regua-comum block now yields no candidate at all, because none of its passing checks accepts that eval's own wrong answers. That is the honest answer and it is the one the old code hid behind a target it should never have named. Four cases, 38 in the file: the failing check reported and not targeted, a target that is a passing check with the arithmetic asserted, no defensible candidate, and checks_to_flip at 4 of 5, 9 of 10 and 8 of 8. Full docs/REPO_HEALTH.md battery green before the commit.
