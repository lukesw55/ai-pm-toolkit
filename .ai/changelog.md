## 2026-09-16: Review: the aggregate verdict becomes an ordered procedure with terms fixed before the run

All three findings land, and the first two defeat the purpose of the section I wrote: it existed to remove post-hoc freedom and it left three kinds of it. Your counter-examples reproduce against my own text. Two skills improving with a non-critical regression in the third satisfies all five Useful conditions and also the Inconclusive "mixed" condition, because "no skill regresses on critical failures" says nothing about a non-critical regression. Critical failures rising with mixed skills satisfies Negative and Inconclusive at once. One skill improving with two unchanged, clean controls, no disagreement and flat cost satisfies none of the three: Useful needs two skills, nothing is mixed or ambiguous so Inconclusive does not fire, and there is a material improvement so Negative does not either. The section is now an ordered procedure, first match wins, with a residual step, so one evidence state maps to exactly one verdict. Step 0 voids an iteration that broke rule 1 or the tag binding, and void is not a result. Step 1 is negative signal on any of: a critical-failure regression; no material improvement alongside a material regression; every material improvement explained by length; no material improvement alongside a cost or latency rise. Step 2 is inconclusive on any of: an unreadable skill; a contaminated pair that would change a direction; a direction resting on a single split pair. Step 3 is useful signal only if all hold: two of the three skills improve materially, no negative control is worse, grader and human agree on every skill. Step 4 makes everything else inconclusive. Your three cases now trace to Useful, Negative and Inconclusive respectively, each exactly once. The load-bearing terms are defined before the run by something observable in the recorded run, not judged in the report. Movement is a change of human verdict category or the appearance or removal of a pre-registered critical failure, so a grader score that moves without changing the category is not movement. A skill improves materially when more of its pairs move up than down and none moves down into a critical failure. An improvement is explained by length when the labeler's own recorded reason cites added length or the assertion's wording rather than the behaviour it names. Grader and human agree when the directions match or the difference does not change the direction. A skill is unreadable when investigate_grader fires and the human verdicts do not settle the direction. A pair is contaminated when its run failed and was retried, or its envelope does not match the pre-registered harness version, model or isolation configuration. You are right that "a judgement recorded with its evidence" documented the judgement without pre-registering the rule behind it, which is exactly where the verdict could still be moved. Cost and latency stop being a free variable: they are always reported, and they reach a verdict only through step 1, whose quality condition is already that nothing improved materially. A cost rise next to a real quality gain is reported and does not make the run negative. On the third finding I picked per harness and said why rather than leaving it open. The comparison is paired inside one harness, the two harnesses run different models, and the protocol already forbids attributing a difference to the harness when the models differ; a single pooled verdict would do precisely that. So each harness iteration gets its own verdict, the summary carries both side by side, a toolkit-level decision requires the same verdict from both, and two differing harness verdicts are not evidence about the harnesses. The active changelog entry carried the old three-bullet semantics and is corrected to the same shape. Against main the protocol file is 66 added lines and no deletions, so nothing from the instrument, tag, smoke or pre-registration contract merged in #26 is touched; the only replaced lines are the ones I added in 18800d0. ## 2026-09-16: EVAL_PROTOCOL: how to read the pilot as a whole

Rule 3 pre-registers a decision per pilot skill, and the report section requires disagreement rates per skill and overall, but nothing said what the pilot as a whole means. Deciding that once the results are in carries the same defect the per-skill pre-registration exists to prevent: the verdict ends up describing the results. The run-level verdict is now pre-registered in the same file and the same commit as the per-skill conditions, before any measured output exists. Three verdicts reached by an ordered procedure, first match wins, so one evidence state maps to exactly one verdict: step 0 voids an iteration that broke rule 1 or the tag binding; step 1 is negative signal on a critical-failure regression, on no material improvement alongside a material regression, on every improvement being explained by length, or on no material improvement alongside a cost or latency rise; step 2 is inconclusive on an unreadable skill, on a contaminated pair that would change a direction, or on a direction resting on a single split pair; step 3 is useful signal only if at least two of the three skills improve materially, no negative control is worse and grader and human agree on every skill; step 4 makes everything else inconclusive, so the procedure is total. No line in it is a statistical test. Every load-bearing term is defined before the run by something observable in the recorded run rather than judged afterwards: movement is a change of human verdict category or the appearance or removal of a pre-registered critical failure; a skill improves materially when more pairs move up than down with none moving down into a critical failure; an improvement is explained by length when the labeler's own recorded reason cites length or assertion wording; a skill is unreadable when investigate_grader fires and the human verdicts do not settle the direction; a pair is contaminated when its run failed and was retried or its envelope does not match the pre-registered harness version, model or isolation configuration. Cost and latency are always reported and can only trigger a verdict through step 1, where the quality condition is already that nothing improved. The report still names which pairs moved and in which direction, per skill, against 6, 5 or 4. The verdict is per harness, never pooled across the two, because the models differ and the protocol forbids attributing a difference to the harness; a toolkit-level decision needs the same verdict from both, and two differing harness verdicts are not evidence about the harnesses. Two sentences carry the weight. Inconclusive is a legitimate outcome and the likely one at this size, not a failure to be argued away. And it does not license a second reading of the same outputs under different criteria: changing the criteria means a new pre-registration and a new iteration, which is rule 1 rather than a new rule. The diff is additive against main, so nothing in the pilot contract is weakened. The real pre-registration file for a measured iteration is still created on the pilot machine, not here.
