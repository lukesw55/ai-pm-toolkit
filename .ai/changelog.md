## 2026-09-16: EVAL_PROTOCOL: how to read the pilot as a whole

Rule 3 pre-registers a decision per pilot skill, and the report section requires disagreement rates per skill and overall, but nothing said what the pilot as a whole means. Deciding that once the results are in carries the same defect the per-skill pre-registration exists to prevent: the verdict ends up describing the results. The run-level verdict is now pre-registered in the same file and the same commit as the per-skill conditions, before any measured output exists. Three verdicts, and no line in them is a statistical test. Useful signal needs all of: at least two of the three skills showing a consistent paired improvement, no skill regressing on critical failures, negative controls not materially worse, the improvement not explained by length alone, and the grader's direction broadly agreeing with the human judgement. Inconclusive follows from any of: mixed gains and regressions, disagreement wide enough that the pairs cannot be read, harness or model failures contaminating pairs that matter, or a reading that depends on a handful of ambiguous cases. Negative signal follows from any of: no material paired improvement anywhere, critical failures increasing, an apparent gain that is mostly verbosity or rubric gaming, or cost and latency rising without a proportional gain in quality. Material is defined as a judgement recorded with its evidence rather than a number. The report names which pairs moved and in which direction, per skill, against that skill's own denominator of 6, 5 or 4, so a reader who disagrees with the verdict can check the pairs instead of arguing with a threshold. That keeps this consistent with the section above it, which already refuses to treat a percentage over four to six cases as an estimate. Two sentences carry the weight. Inconclusive is a legitimate outcome and the likely one at this size, not a failure to be argued away. And it does not license a second reading of the same outputs under different criteria: changing the criteria means a new pre-registration and a new iteration, which is rule 1 rather than a new rule. The diff is 41 added lines and no deletions, so nothing in the pilot contract is weakened. The real pre-registration file for a measured iteration is still created on the pilot machine, not here.
